##### Summary

The UN Framework Convention on International Tax Cooperation puts a decision in front of the world: whether to keep taxing multinationals where they declare their profits, or to tax them where their real economic activity takes place. This report quantifies what that decision is worth. Using aggregate country-by-country reporting data published by the OECD, we estimate the revenue effects of unitary taxation with formulary apportionment, under which a multinational group is taxed as a single unit and its global profits are shared between countries according to a formula of economic activity.

Had taxing rights been allocated by where workers are located and where sales are made, the world would collectively have raised between US$300 billion and over US$500 billion in additional tax revenue in each year for which data are available, without any increase in tax rates. Revenue shifts away from the jurisdictions where profits are currently booked and towards the countries where the activity happens, so that apart from corporate tax havens almost every country gains. The report also shows how results change when countries' resource rights are treated as prior to taxing rights, reports alternative apportionment formulas, and identifies the small group of countries that would lose and how those losses could be addressed. Country-level results for all formulas can be explored in the interactive data explorer linked above.
